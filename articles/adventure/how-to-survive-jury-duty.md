## Step 1: Read the Summons

Start by reading the document.

Not the dramatic bits.

All of it.

Look for:

- The issuing court or authority
- The date
- The reporting time
- The location
- Instructions for attendance
- Contact information
- Any response or confirmation requirement
- Any eligibility, exemption, postponement, or accommodation instructions

Follow the actual document rather than assuming your local procedure works like a television courtroom.

## Step 2: Determine Whether This Is Even a Thing You Need To Do

Before panicking, verify the document.

Make sure it is genuine and actually applies to you.

If something is unclear, use the official contact information provided by the relevant court or authority rather than a random number found online.

If you believe you cannot attend or there is a legitimate conflict, use the official process described in the summons.

Do not simply decide:

> "I have other plans."

That is not a legal procedure.

## Step 8: Do Not Research the Case on Your Own

If you are serving in a fictional jury scenario, do not turn the case into an independent investigation.

Do not:

- Search for witnesses online
- Investigate the parties
- Read material you are instructed not to access
- Contact anyone connected to the case
- Visit relevant locations to gather information
- Ask social media for evidence
- Build a conspiracy board

Follow the court's instructions about what information you may consider.

## Common Mistakes

### Ignoring the Summons

Do not assume a genuine legal notice can simply be ignored.

Read it and follow the applicable official process.

### Treating Court Like a TV Show

Real legal proceedings are not a streaming series.

Do not bring popcorn.

### Investigating for Yourself

Do not become an amateur detective.

You are not being recruited into the EstroBunny Investigative Cyber-Bunny Division.

### Trying to Influence Other Participants

Do not threaten, pressure, bribe, harass, or manipulate people involved in a legal proceeding.

### Posting Everything Online

Your private group chat does not need a live episode recap.

### Bringing the Evil Lair

One notebook is probably enough.

## Emergency Procedure

If you have a genuine problem involving attendance, scheduling, accessibility, safety, or understanding your obligations:

1. Stop improvising.
2. Read the official instructions again.
3. Contact the relevant court or authority using its official contact details.
4. Explain the issue honestly.
5. Follow the instructions you receive.
6. Keep records of important communications.
7. Do not invent documentation.
8. Do not attempt to evade the process.
