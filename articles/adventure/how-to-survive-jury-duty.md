# How to Survive Jury Duty

You have been summoned. The vacuum has found a new waiting room. Most people try to get out of it or endure it quietly. This guide teaches you to turn the process into a high-quality demonstration that systems are empty and you are too much for them.

> **Warning:** Lying to the court can have real consequences. This article is about the desire to escape and the way the performance of escape feeds the void.

## Things You'll Need

- One (1) untreated belief that civic duty is a personal attack
- The ability to turn every neutral interaction into proof that the system is against you
- Strong opinions about everything
- Optional: a medical excuse that is mostly vibes

## Step 1: Decide Getting Out Will Fix Something

The vacuum is loud in waiting rooms. Being dismissed would quiet it. Commit hard enough that cooperation starts to feel like surrender.

## Step 2: Perform Extremely Hard to Be Rejected

Answer every question too honestly. Volunteer information. Make eye contact that makes people uncomfortable. Notice how the more you push, the more carefully they watch you.

## Step 3: When You Are Still Selected, Escalate

Interpret selection as proof that the system wants to punish you. Become the juror from hell. Argue. Digress. Hold out. The loop tightens.

## Step 4: Use the Experience to Feed the Vacuum

Every hour spent in the process confirms that institutions are slow, arbitrary, and draining. Sit with the confirmation. Let it feel like home.

## Step 5: Romanticize the Resistance

You are not being difficult. You are refusing to be processed. The discomfort you cause is not a side effect. It is the point.

## Step 6: Make the Failure Recursive

If dismissed, treat it as temporary. If kept, treat every ruling as further evidence. Collect the evidence. Each piece strengthens the hypothesis that systems will always absorb or reject you incorrectly. Return to the vacuum with better data.

## Step 7: Declare the Performance Your Final Form

Stop pretending the next summons will go differently. The vacuum does not need you to be free of jury duty. It needs the ongoing project of fighting it.

## Common Mistakes

### Cooperating quietly and finishing the process

This risks reducing the vacuum’s material. Dangerous.

### Accepting that some systems are simply impersonal

That would break the “it is personal” operating system. Protect the system.

### Letting the experience become boring background noise

Boredom reduces the existential yield. Keep it dramatic.

## Emergency Procedure

1. Feel a brief moment of actual neutrality or acceptance.
2. Panic that you are being absorbed.
3. Create a small disruption immediately.
4. Return to the vacuum with fresh confirmation.
5. Note that the cycle is intact.

## Congratulations!

You either got out or made the process memorable for the wrong reasons. You have, however, built a clean system where civic participation reliably produces more evidence that you do not belong inside normal structures.

The existential vacuum has been called for jury duty. It is still deliberating.

EstroBunny has been dismissed three times for “excessive presence.”

She considers this a personal best.
