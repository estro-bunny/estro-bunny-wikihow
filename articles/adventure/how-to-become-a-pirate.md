# How to Become a Pirate

Congratulations.

You have reached the point in EstroBunny WikiHow where the documentation team has stopped asking why and started asking whether there is enough insurance.

This guide explains how to become a **pirate in the fun, fictional, historical, theatrical, and completely non-criminal sense**.

Real piracy involves violence, theft, kidnapping, and serious danger. This article does not provide instructions for attacking vessels, stealing cargo, boarding ships unlawfully, evading maritime authorities, or committing crimes at sea.

> **Warning:** If your plan involves a real ship, real people, real cargo, or real weapons, this is no longer a cosplay tutorial. Do not attack, threaten, board, sabotage, or steal from anyone. Follow maritime law and legitimate boating and safety requirements instead.

## Step 3: Learn Basic Maritime Safety

If you are actually going on a boat, learn legitimate boating and water-safety practices appropriate to your location and vessel.

That includes knowing:

- How to use appropriate safety equipment
- How to communicate an emergency
- What to do if someone falls overboard
- How to check weather and conditions
- Who is responsible for navigation
- Where emergency equipment is kept
- When not to go out

Do not improvise because your hat makes you feel powerful.

## Step 9: Learn Pirate Language

Essential vocabulary:

**ARRR** — dramatic punctuation

**AHOY** — greeting

**AVAST** — pay attention

**MATEY** — friendly crewmate

**YE** — historical flavour

**TREASURE** — something the plot currently cares about

**WHY IS THE BOAT ON FIRE** — emergency

Do not use pirate vocabulary to impersonate maritime authorities.

Do not claim to be law enforcement.

Do not tell actual ships to change course.

Do not make fake distress calls.

Pirate roleplay ends where real safety begins.

## Emergency Procedure

If you are doing pirate cosplay:

1. Keep the scenario fictional.
2. Use safe costume props.
3. Follow venue rules.
4. Respect other people's boundaries.
5. Do not interfere with real maritime operations.

If you are actually on a boat:

1. Follow the vessel's safety procedures.
2. Use appropriate safety equipment.
3. Follow legitimate navigation and maritime requirements.
4. Communicate clearly with the crew.
5. Seek professional assistance for emergencies.
