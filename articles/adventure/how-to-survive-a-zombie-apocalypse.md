# How to Survive a Zombie Apocalypse

Good news.

The zombies are fictional.

Better news: emergency preparedness is not.

This guide uses the zombie apocalypse as an excuse to prepare for boring things such as power outages, storms, evacuations, injuries, and other emergencies that are considerably less likely to moan outside your window.

The CDC has literally used zombies as a preparedness teaching device, recommending basics such as water, food, medications, first aid supplies, lighting, communications, important documents, and an emergency plan. citeturn0search2turn0search12

> **Warning:** This is an emergency-preparedness guide wearing a zombie costume. It does not provide instructions for fighting, hunting, trapping, or harming people. If the apocalypse is fictional, enjoy the fiction. If an actual emergency happens, follow local authorities and real emergency guidance.

## Step 1: Confirm That the Zombies Are Actually Zombies

Before declaring the apocalypse, establish what is happening.

Possible explanations include:

- A zombie outbreak
- A power outage
- A costume convention
- A movie shoot
- Your neighbor walking strangely
- EstroBunny discovering coffee at 03:00

Do not immediately announce:

> "THE DEAD HAVE RISEN."

Especially if you have not checked.

## Step 2: Build the Emergency Kit

This is where the joke becomes suspiciously practical.

Keep enough essential supplies for your household and your circumstances.

Include food and water, medications, first aid, lighting, communications, hygiene supplies, important documents, and anything required for disability, mobility, childcare, or pets. citeturn0search12turn0search3

## Step 3: Make an Emergency Plan

Choose:

- A household meeting place
- Emergency contacts
- A way to communicate if phones fail
- An evacuation destination
- More than one route to get there
- A plan for pets and anyone who needs additional support

The CDC's zombie preparedness guidance similarly recommends identifying possible emergencies, meeting places, contacts, and evacuation routes. citeturn0search13

Do not make the plan:

```text
IF ZOMBIES:
    RUN
```

That is not a plan.

That is a panic response with indentation.

## Step 4: Know Your Local Area

Learn where you are.

Identify:

- Emergency services
- Safe meeting places
- Major roads
- Alternative routes
- Places where official emergency information is provided
- Areas prone to flooding, fires, storms, or other local hazards

Use an actual map.

## Step 5: Protect the Boring Things

Keep your phone charged when possible.

Keep important documents accessible.

Maintain necessary medications.

Know how to shut off utilities if you have been instructed and it is safe to do so.

Keep emergency contacts written down.

Back up important digital information.

Keep some emergency supplies in an appropriate place away from obvious hazards.

## Step 6: Do Not Become the Main Character

Zombie movies encourage certain behaviors.

These include:

- Splitting up unnecessarily
- Investigating mysterious noises
- Saying "I'll be right back"
- Walking into abandoned buildings
- Ignoring obvious warning signs
- Making extremely confident plans with zero evidence

Do not copy these behaviors.

In a real emergency, prioritize safety, communication, official information, and getting yourself and others to an appropriate safe location.

## Step 8: Choose a Safe Shelter

A good emergency shelter should be appropriate to the actual hazard.

Depending on the situation, that may mean staying home, moving to a designated shelter, evacuating, or following instructions from emergency authorities.

Do not assume one location is universally safe.

A basement may be useful for one hazard and dangerous for another.

A beach house may be excellent during a zombie movie.

It may be less excellent during a severe storm.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake: Waiting Until the Emergency Starts

Preparedness works better when done before you need it.

### Mistake: Packing Only Snacks

Snacks are important.

They are not a complete emergency plan.

### Mistake: Forgetting Medication

Account for necessary medications and medical supplies when building your kit. citeturn0search0turn0search1

### Mistake: Forgetting Pets

Pets need food, water, medication, identification, and appropriate carriers or supplies as applicable. citeturn0search1

### Mistake: Trusting One Route

Have alternatives.

### Mistake: Becoming the Main Character

Stop.

### Mistake: Letting EstroBunny Design the Evacuation Plan

## Emergency Procedure

If a real emergency happens:

1. Assess immediate danger.
2. Follow local emergency instructions.
3. Contact emergency services when appropriate.
4. Use your emergency kit.
5. Communicate with your household or support network.
6. Evacuate or shelter according to official guidance.
7. Help others when it is safe to do so.
8. Conserve essential resources.
9. Monitor reliable updates.
10. Do not improvise a zombie battle because you saw it in a movie.

If there are actual people outside:

They are people until you have reliable reason to believe otherwise.

Do not attack people because you think they are zombies.

Do not create danger for yourself or others trying to investigate.
