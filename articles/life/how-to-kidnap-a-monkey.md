# How to Kidnap a Monkey

You want a monkey. This is the vacuum looking for a mascot. Most people try to fill emptiness with pets, projects, or other humans. This guide teaches you to chase a monkey until the chase itself becomes the emptiness.

> **Warning:** Do not kidnap real monkeys. This article is about the desire, the failure, and the way the failure feeds the void. The plush version is the only legal ending.

## Things You'll Need

- One (1) untreated urge to acquire something that cannot love you back
- The ability to turn every failed plan into proof that nothing works
- A banana you will never use correctly
- Optional: a cage that stays empty on purpose

## Step 1: Decide the Monkey Will Fix Something

The vacuum is loud. A monkey would make it quieter. Or funnier. Or at least different. Commit to the idea hard enough that it starts to feel like a solution.

## Step 2: Build Plans That Cannot Succeed

Draw diagrams. Watch videos. Research nothing practical. The more elaborate the plan, the safer it is from ever being tested. The fantasy stays pure.

## Step 3: Approach the Edge of Action and Stop

Get close enough to feel the risk. Then abort. Notice the relief mixed with disappointment. The vacuum notes both feelings and keeps them.

## Step 4: Replace the Real Thing with a Symbol

Buy the plush monkey. Name it. Talk to it. Blame it. Photograph it. Let it become the official representative of the thing you will never have.

## Step 5: Use the Symbol to Feed the Vacuum

Every time you look at the plush monkey, remember the real one you did not get. The gap between the object and the desire becomes a reliable source of emptiness. Perfect.

## Step 6: Make the Failure Recursive

Tell the story of almost getting a monkey. Watch people back away. Interpret their discomfort as further proof that your desires are too much or too strange. Return to the plush monkey with stronger evidence that connection (even with animals) is impossible.

## Step 7: Declare the Empty Cage Your Final Form

Stop pretending the next plan will work. The vacuum does not need a monkey. It needs the ongoing project of not having one.

## Common Mistakes

### Actually acquiring a real animal

This would introduce new problems and possibly reduce the vacuum. Dangerous.

### Letting the desire die quietly

A dead desire cannot feed the loop. Keep it on life support.

### Accepting that some wants are better left as wants

That would be wisdom. We are not here for wisdom.

## Emergency Procedure

1. Feel a brief urge to let the monkey fantasy go.
2. Panic at the loss of a project.
3. Restart the planning phase immediately.
4. Return to the vacuum with a fresh diagram.
5. Note that the cycle is intact.

## Congratulations!

You still do not have a monkey. You have, however, built a clean system where the desire for a monkey reliably produces more emptiness, more storytelling, and more proof that acquisition is impossible.

The existential vacuum has a mascot now. It is a plush monkey named after the thing it is not.

EstroBunny has filed the incident report.

The monkey remains free.

The vacuum remains employed.
