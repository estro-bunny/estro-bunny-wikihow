# How to Fix a Broken Back

If by “broken back” you mean a suspected spinal fracture or serious spinal injury, do not attempt to fix it yourself. A damaged spine is not a loose USB cable. There is no safe `unplug → plug back in` procedure.

This guide covers what to do when someone may have seriously injured their back, followed by the much less dramatic situation of ordinary back pain. The first objective is not to repair the person. It is to avoid making the injury worse while getting appropriate medical care.

> **Warning:** If there was a significant accident, severe back or neck pain, weakness, numbness, tingling, loss of movement, difficulty walking, unusual deformity, altered consciousness, breathing problems, or other signs of a serious spinal injury, treat it as an emergency and contact your local emergency service. ## Step 1: Determine Whether This Is Actually an Emergency

First, stop and look at what happened.

A suspected spinal injury can follow events such as a major vehicle collision, a significant fall, a high-impact sports injury, or another forceful impact. Concerning signs include severe back or neck pain, weakness, numbness, tingling, loss of movement, difficulty walking, deformity, changes in alertness, or impaired breathing. citeturn0search2turn0search26

## Step 2: Do Not Try to Straighten the Spine

If you suspect a spinal injury, tell the person to stay still and remain in the position they were found in unless there is an immediate danger, CPR is required, or another situation makes movement unavoidable. Do not ask them to twist, bend, stand, walk, or “test” the injury. citeturn0search0turn0search2

Do not attempt to:

- Crack the back
- Pull on the person
- Twist the torso
- Force the spine into alignment
- Massage the injured area
- Make the person stand up
- Perform an improvised spinal adjustment
- Ask whether they can “just walk it off”

## Step 3: Call for Medical Help

If a serious spinal injury is suspected, contact your local emergency medical service and clearly say that a spinal or back injury is suspected.

Give them the important information:

- What happened
- Where the person is
- Whether they are conscious
- Whether they are breathing normally
- Whether they can move their arms and legs
- Whether they report numbness, tingling, weakness, or severe pain
- Any other obvious injuries

Then follow the dispatcher's instructions.

## Step 6: If It Is Ordinary Back Pain, Use a Different Strategy

Not every painful back is a broken spine.

For uncomplicated back pain without red-flag symptoms, recovery often involves staying gently active rather than remaining in bed for long periods. Short periods of rest can help when pain is severe, but prolonged bed rest can contribute to stiffness and is generally discouraged. citeturn0search10turn0search11

Depending on the situation, reasonable self-care may include:

- Gentle movement
- Short walks
- Changing position regularly
- Avoiding activities that clearly aggravate the pain
- Using an ice or heat pack appropriately
- Asking a pharmacist or clinician which pain relief is suitable for you
- Getting medical advice if symptoms are worsening or not improving

Do not confuse “stay gently active” with “deadlift your washing machine.”

These are different concepts.

## Step 7: Know the Red Flags

Seek urgent medical help for concerning neurological symptoms such as new weakness, numbness, tingling, difficulty walking, or loss of sensation, particularly after an injury. Severe symptoms after a significant accident also warrant urgent assessment. citeturn0search2turn0search5

Some back-pain symptoms involving bladder, bowel, or sexual function can also indicate a serious neurological problem and require urgent assessment. citeturn0search10

If you are unsure whether the situation is serious, get medical advice rather than trying to diagnose a spinal injury from a search engine.

## Step 8: Let the Professionals Decide What Is Broken

A suspected spinal fracture or serious back injury may require examination and medical imaging. The appropriate treatment depends on the actual injury.

Possible management can range from activity guidance and pain control to specialist treatment. There is no universal “fix a broken back” procedure because “broken back” is not a diagnosis precise enough to determine treatment.

## Emergency Procedure

If someone may have a serious spinal injury:

1. Make sure the scene is safe.
2. Do not ask them to move.
3. Call your local emergency service.
4. Tell responders that a spinal injury is suspected.
5. Keep the person as still as possible in the position found unless immediate danger, CPR, or another life-threatening situation requires movement.
6. Monitor breathing and responsiveness.
7. Follow the emergency operator's instructions.
8. Do not attempt to straighten, crack, massage, or manipulate the spine. citeturn0search0turn0search2
