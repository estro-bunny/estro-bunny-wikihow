# How to Make Friends

Making friends is a simple process.

Unfortunately, humans have made it weird.

The basic procedure is:

1. Meet people.
2. Talk to them.
3. Find something you enjoy together.
4. Keep talking.
5. Spend time together.
6. Respect each other.
7. Congratulations: you have friends.

Friendships can support belonging, self-worth, emotional support, and mental wellbeing. Joining groups around shared interests, volunteering, and making regular contact can all create opportunities to build connections. citeturn0search0turn0search2turn0search5

> **Warning:** You cannot force someone to become your friend. Friendship is a mutual relationship. If someone is not interested, respect the answer and continue your search for compatible humans.

## Step 2: Find Your People

You do not need to become friends with everyone.

Look for people who share some combination of:

- Interests
- Humor
- Values
- Hobbies
- Communication style
- General tolerance for nonsense

Shared interests are useful because they give you something to talk about without requiring you to immediately explain your entire personality.

Try:

> “Wait, you play that too?”

> “How did you get into that?”

> “Have you tried the new update?”

> “I love that.”

These are conversations.

You have successfully discovered speech.

## Step 3: Say Hello

This is the terrifying part.

Say hello.

Not:

> “Greetings. I have identified you as a potential friendship candidate.”

Just:

> “Hey!”

Then talk about whatever is happening around you.

Ask a question.

Listen to the answer.

Share something of your own.

Good conversation is not an interrogation. It is a back-and-forth.

Active listening—paying attention, asking follow-up questions, and checking that you understood someone correctly—can strengthen relationships. citeturn0search0

## Step 4: Do Something Together

Friendships become easier to build when you actually spend time together.

Try something low-pressure:

- Play a game
- Get coffee
- Study together
- Go for a walk
- Watch something
- Work on a project
- Visit an event
- Share a hobby
- Sit together in a voice call
- Send each other memes

The activity does not have to be impressive.

Two people eating chips while complaining about a game counts.

EstroBunny has confirmed this through extensive research.

Her sample size is one.

## Step 5: Follow Up

This is where many potential friendships mysteriously disappear.

You had a good conversation.

Then nobody messages anyone for six months.

Do not do this.

Send the message.

> “Hey, that was fun yesterday!”

> “Want to play again sometime?”

> “I found that thing we were talking about.”

> “Are you around this weekend?”

Regular contact helps relationships develop. Reconnecting with old friends can also be worthwhile rather than assuming a friendship is permanently over. citeturn0search0turn0search14

## Step 6: Remember Things About Them

If someone tells you something important, remember it.

You do not need a 900-page database.

Just remember:

- Their interests
- Things they are working on
- Important events
- Their favorite things
- Things they mentioned wanting to try

Then ask about them later.

> “How did your exam go?”

> “Did you ever finish that game?”

> “How's your new job?”

## Step 7: Be A Good Friend

Making friends is only half the problem.

Keeping them requires being someone other people can actually rely on.

Try to:

- Listen
- Show up when you reasonably can
- Celebrate their good news
- Support them when things are difficult
- Respect their boundaries
- Keep appropriate confidences
- Apologize when you mess up
- Let them disagree with you
- Make space for their interests too

Healthy friendships involve respect, support, communication, and room for disagreement without humiliation or coercion. citeturn0search4turn0search15

Do not make every friendship transaction-based.

Friendship is not:

`YOU HELPED ME WITH CSS = I OWE YOU ONE SOUL`

## Step 9: Don't Try To Collect Humans

You are not building a Pokémon team.

Do not approach every person in a room with:

> “Would you like to join my social network?”

You can have different levels of friendship.

Some people are:

- Acquaintances
- Gaming friends
- Hobby friends
- Close friends
- Online friends
- Friends you see occasionally
- People you have not spoken to in three years but somehow immediately continue talking to

Not every connection needs to become your Best Friend Forever™.

Quality matters more than assembling the largest possible social inventory.

## Step 10: Make Online Friends Safely

Online friendships can be genuine and meaningful, but normal safety rules still apply.

Do not send money, banking information, passwords, or other sensitive information to people you do not know and trust. Be cautious when moving online friendships into real-world meetings; if you meet someone in person, use sensible safety precautions such as choosing a public place and telling someone you trust where you are. citeturn0search5

## Step 11: Accept That Not Everyone Will Click

Sometimes you meet someone.

You talk.

You try.

And it does not become a friendship.

That does not necessarily mean anyone did anything wrong.

People have different personalities, schedules, interests, and social needs.

If someone does not want to continue a friendship, respect that.

Do not:

- Harass them
- Demand an explanation
- Contact their friends for an explanation
- Create a revenge campaign
- Make a 46-page manifesto
- Appear outside their house holding a friendship application

Move on.

There are other humans.

Probably millions of them.

One of them might even like your terrible jokes.

## Step 12: Maintain The Friendship

Friendships need maintenance.

Not corporate maintenance.

Human maintenance.

That means:

- Check in occasionally
- Make plans
- Remember important things
- Make time when possible
- Communicate when you are busy
- Say when something bothered you
- Give each other space
- Reconnect after long gaps

You do not have to talk every day to be friends.

Some friendships survive long periods of silence and resume almost immediately.

## Common Mistakes

### Waiting For Someone Else To Make The First Move

Sometimes you have to say hello.

### Talking Only About Yourself

Conversation is not a podcast.

### Treating Every Interaction Like A Test

Nobody is grading you.

### Oversharing Immediately

Vulnerability is good.

Dumping your entire autobiography onto someone you met fourteen seconds ago is a different activity.

### Never Following Up

Good conversation can disappear if nobody makes another effort.

### Trying Too Hard

You do not need to perform friendship.

### Assuming Loneliness Means Something Is Wrong With You

Loneliness can happen for many reasons, including life changes, moving, social anxiety, or simply not having enough opportunities to connect. citeturn0search2turn0search3

### Making One Person Your Entire Support System

Friends are important.

One person should not have to become your entire social infrastructure.

## Emergency Procedure

If you have attempted to make friends and the situation has escalated beyond reasonable social behavior:

1. Put down the phone.
2. Stop sending messages.
3. Do not stalk their social media.
4. Do not manufacture coincidences.
5. Do not create a spreadsheet ranking your friends.
6. Talk to someone you already trust.
7. Do something you enjoy.
8. Try another social environment later.
9. Remember that one awkward interaction is not a life sentence.
10. Try again.
