## Step 2: Have an Actual Reason to Start a Conversation

Good reasons include:

- You share a class or workplace.
- You're both attending the same event.
- You have a shared hobby.
- You are playing the same game.
- You want to ask a normal question.
- She has mentioned something you genuinely find interesting.
- You are already part of the same conversation.

Reasonable opening:

> "Hey, I noticed you're playing [game]. How are you finding it?"

Also reasonable:

> "Hey, how's your day going?"

Less reasonable:

> "Greetings, female human. I have completed my research."

## Step 4: Ask Questions You Actually Care About

Good questions are open enough to create conversation.

Examples:

- "What games have you been playing lately?"
- "How did you get into that hobby?"
- "What kind of music do you like?"
- "Have you watched anything good recently?"
- "How did you end up here?"
- "What have you been working on?"

Then listen.

Do not immediately turn the answer into a speech about yourself.

Example:

**Person:** "I've been playing a lot of Stardew Valley."

**Bad response:** "Actually, I have 900 hours and here is my complete agricultural theory."

**Better response:** "Oh nice. What farm layout are you using?"

## Step 5: Notice Whether the Conversation Is Mutual

A conversation is not a hostage negotiation.

Look for ordinary signs that someone is participating:

- They ask questions back.
- They continue the conversation.
- They volunteer information.
- They seem comfortable.
- They engage with the topic.

If someone gives short answers, seems uncomfortable, repeatedly tries to leave, or clearly does not want to continue, give them space.

Do not treat disinterest as a puzzle.

Do not attempt to unlock the hidden dialogue option.

## Step 11: Respect Boundaries

Everyone gets to have boundaries.

That includes:

- Physical boundaries
- Emotional boundaries
- Sexual boundaries
- Time and attention
- Privacy
- Personal information
- Online communication

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake: Treating Women Like a Single Category

There is no universal personality called Woman.

Individual people differ.

### Mistake: Talking Only About Yourself

Conversation requires two people.

Ask questions and listen.

### Mistake: Waiting for the Perfect Line

The perfect line is usually just a normal sentence.

### Mistake: Interpreting Every Kindness as Romantic Interest

Someone being friendly does not automatically mean they want a relationship.

### Mistake: Ignoring Discomfort

If someone seems uncomfortable, give them space.

### Mistake: Taking Rejection Personally as an Attack

Someone can like you as a person and still not want to date you.

### Mistake: Using Manipulation

Do not manipulate, pressure, guilt, threaten, or deceive someone into giving you attention or affection.

### Mistake: Assuming Experience Makes You a Mind Reader

It does not.

Ask.

Listen.

Communicate.
